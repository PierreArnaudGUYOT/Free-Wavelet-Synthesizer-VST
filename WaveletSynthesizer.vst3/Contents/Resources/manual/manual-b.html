WAVELET SYNTHESIZER · USER MANUAL B

ENGINE BWavelet Additive

Engine B builds sounds the other way round. Instead of starting from a waveform and taking detail away, you write the detail directly. An ATOM PATTERN decides the sign — and for RANDOM, the size — of every one of the 2047 atoms that make up a waveform cycle; the WAVELET gives those atoms their shape; eleven DRAWBARS set the level of each band, one octave of harmonics each, like the drawbars of an organ; and a SCALE ENVELOPE gives every band its own attack and decay, so the fine detail can bloom later or die sooner than the body of the sound. The shared SCALE FILTER, POSITION window, envelopes, LFO, voice and effects sections then shape the result.

1The idea in one minute

Partials versus atoms

Classic additive synthesis builds a sound from sine waves: one oscillator per harmonic, each with its own level and envelope. It is powerful but laborious — a bright tone needs dozens of partials — and every partial lasts for the whole cycle, so there is no way to say where in the waveform something happens.

Engine B adds up wavelet atoms instead. An atom is a short wiggle of a fixed shape — the wavelet — and atoms come in sizes an octave apart: one atom spanning the whole cycle, two spanning half of it each, then four, eight … down to 1024 tiny atoms spaced two samples apart. All atoms of one size form a band, and each band covers about one octave of harmonics. Instead of setting dozens of sine levels you set eleven band levels, and the pattern of the atoms inside each band decides the waveform.

  • One cycle = 2048 samples = 2047 atoms (1 + 2 + 4 + … + 1024) in 11 bands.
  • Band b has 2b−1 atoms. Band 1 is about the fundamental, band 2 the 2nd harmonic, band 3 harmonics 3–4, band 4 harmonics 5–8 … each band is about one octave of harmonics.
  • The ATOM PATTERN sets the sign (and for RANDOM the size) of every atom, the WAVELET sets their shape, and the DRAWBARS, TILT, SCALE ENVELOPE and SCALE FILTER set the level of each band.

Engine B turns each band into its own waveform layer, scales it so that its peak is exactly 1, and mixes the 11 layers at audio rate with the band levels you set.

To keep high notes free of aliasing, each band layer is stored in eleven band-limited versions, one per octave, and every note uses the version whose harmonics stay below the sample rate minus 19 kHz (about 25 kHz at 44.1 kHz, 29 kHz at 48 kHz) — anything that folds back lands above 19 kHz. The consequence is musical: the finest bands only exist on low notes. Every octave down adds one band. At 48 kHz, band 7 is the highest band played for notes from about A♯3 to A4, band 8 an octave lower, band 9 two octaves lower; band 10 needs notes below about 57 Hz and band 11 below 28 Hz. (At 44.1 kHz the limits are about a whole tone lower.)

TIP

The drawbars follow the same rule. At middle C only drawbars 1–7 are heard; drawbars 8–11 add detail to bass notes only. When you build a registration, play it across the keyboard.

The wavelet chooses the atom

The WAVELET selector (shared by both engines) decides what the atoms look like — and in engine B, the colour of every band. Seven classic wavelets are available, from the 2-tap Haar step to the 40-tap Daubechies 20:

Signal flow

  • Table building happens only when you change PATTERN, SEED or WAVELET: the pattern writes the atoms, an inverse wavelet transform turns each band into a waveform layer, and each layer is normalised to a peak of 1. The switch to new tables is crossfaded, so you can change these controls while playing.
  • Every voice plays the 11 layers at its own pitch and mixes them with gains made of the drawbar level, the TILT, the band’s SCALE ENVELOPE and the SCALE FILTER. The POSITION window can then restrict bands 2–11 to part of the cycle.
  • All voices are summed and pass through the saturation, delay and reverb, a DC blocker, the master volume and a soft limiter that keeps the output below 0 dBFS.
A BEAUTIFUL CONSEQUENCE

A sawtooth built from square waves

With the Haar wavelet and the RAMP pattern every band is a square wave: band 1 at the fundamental, band 2 at twice that frequency, band 3 at four times … each one twice as fast as the one below. Now set TILT to −6 dB per band, which halves the level from one band to the next. Squares whose frequency doubles while their level halves add up to a staircase that gets finer with every band — and converges to a perfect sawtooth. The −6 dB per octave of the tilt is exactly the 1/n law of a sawtooth’s harmonics: measured over the first 16 harmonics, the full eleven-band staircase matches a sawtooth to within a fraction of a decibel.

That is the preset B · Init. Pull the drawbars down from the right and the steps get coarser; drawbar 1 alone is a plain square wave.

Atom patterns

The PATTERN buttons fill the 2047 atoms with one of six recipes. The pattern decides which harmonics each band contains and how they line up in time; the wavelet decides how sharp or smooth each atom is.

PatternAtomsSound
RAMPEvery atom +1.Each band repeats its atom over and over, so it sounds at exactly one harmonic: band b at harmonic 2b−1 (1, 2, 4, 8 …). With long wavelets these are almost pure sines — octave drawbars; with Haar they are square waves.
MIRRORBand 1: +1. Other bands: first half of the atoms +1, second half −1.The second half of the cycle becomes an upside-down copy of the first, which cancels every even harmonic: hollow, square- and clarinet-like tones.
ALTThe sign alternates from band to band: bands 1, 3, 5 … +1, bands 2, 4, 6 … −1.The same harmonic levels as RAMP with different phases. A steady tone sounds very close to RAMP, but the waveform differs — which you hear as soon as the POSITION window cuts into the cycle.

IMPULSEOnly the first atom of each band is on.All the detail sits at the very start of the cycle: a click at the beginning of every period, on top of band 1. NOTE

After the transform, every band layer is scaled so that its peak is exactly 1. A drawbar at 100 % therefore always means “this band at full scale”, whatever the pattern, and the patterns differ in tone colour rather than in level.

Drawbars and tilt

The eleven drawbars set the level of each band, much like the drawbars of a tonewheel organ — except that each drawbar is a whole octave of harmonics rather than a single sine. With the RAMP pattern and a long wavelet they really are octave-pitched organ drawbars:

There are no fifths or thirds (the 5⅓′, 2⅔′, 1⅗′ and 1⅓′ drawbars of a tonewheel organ), because every band is an octave. With Haar, each band is a square wave and adds its odd multiples — a reedier sound.

TILT adds a slope across the drawbars: band 1 is the reference and every band above gets TILT dB more (or less) than the one below. At −6 dB/band the level halves from band to band; at 0 all drawbars count equally; positive values brighten the sound and are best used sparingly (+1 to +3), because at +12 dB/band band 11 would be 120 dB above band 1.

Level normalisation. To keep the output out of clipping, the mix is divided by the sum of the drawbar levels (after TILT) whenever that sum is above 1. With TILT −6 dB/band and all drawbars up the sum is about 2; with TILT 0 and all drawbars up it is 11, so each band plays at 1/11 of full scale. Two consequences: pulling one drawbar down makes the others a little louder, so the overall level stays similar; and a flat, all-up registration is quieter than you might expect — raise VOLUME or use a negative TILT.

UNDER THE HOOD
gain(b) = DRAWBAR(b) × 10^(TILT × (b − 1) / 20) × scale envelope(b) × filter(b) ÷ max(1, Σ DRAWBAR × 10^(TILT × (b − 1) / 20))

The scale envelope and the SCALE FILTER are not part of the divisor, so they make the sound quieter and darker exactly as you would expect.

Scale envelope

The SCALE ENVELOPE gives every band its own envelope. When a note starts, all bands begin at zero and rise linearly to full level, each in its own attack time; then each decays exponentially, at its own speed, towards SUSTAIN, where it stays while the key is held. There is no separate release: when you let go, the AMP ENVELOPE fades the whole sound while the bands keep moving towards SUSTAIN.

  • ATTACK and SPREAD. Band b rises in ATTACK × 16SPREAD × (t − ½), where t runs from 0 for band 1 to 1 for band 11. At SPREAD 0 all bands rise together in ATTACK. At +100 % band 1 takes ATTACK ÷ 4, band 6 ATTACK and band 11 ATTACK × 4: the sound opens as the detail arrives. At −100 % the detail comes first and the body follows.
  • BODY and BRIGHT. Band 1 decays with BODY, band 11 with BRIGHT, and the bands in between are spaced evenly on a logarithmic scale. With BODY 3 s and BRIGHT 0.4 s: band 1 3.0 s, band 3 2.0 s, band 6 1.1 s, band 9 0.6 s, band 11 0.4 s. Times are measured to −60 dB of the distance to SUSTAIN.
  • SUSTAIN is the level every band settles at; 100 % means no decay at all.
  • The defaults — ATTACK 5 ms, SPREAD 0 %, BODY and BRIGHT 20 s, SUSTAIN 100 % — make the scale envelope practically neutral: a 5 ms fade-in.
  • Real strings, bells and plucked sounds lose their upper harmonics faster than their fundamental. A BRIGHT time shorter than BODY reproduces exactly that, and is why engine B plucks and bells sound physically plausible.
  • A note played legato in mono mode does not restart the scale envelope; a voice that is re-triggered while still sounding restarts its attacks from the current band levels.
  • The SCOPE and SCALOGRAM follow the scale envelope live while you play.

5Scale filter

In engine B the SCALE FILTER multiplies the band levels set by the drawbars, the TILT and the SCALE ENVELOPE. Think of it as a second set of drawbars that you can move with an envelope, an LFO, velocity and the keyboard. Its default CUTOFF of 12 is fully open, so you can ignore it until you want brightness that changes over time or with playing strength.

The level normalisation of engine B (see Drawbars and tilt) is computed before the filter, so closing the filter really makes the sound darker and quieter — exactly as you would expect from a subtractive filter.

The four types in numbers

  • Low-pass — bands below the cutoff pass untouched; the band at the cutoff is about 0.17 × SLOPE down (−4 dB at 24 dB/band); each band further up loses about SLOPE dB more. At CUTOFF 12 (the default) every band is within 0.1 dB at 24 dB/band (0.4 dB at 96 dB/band): fully open.
  • High-pass — the mirror image: bands above the cutoff pass, bands below lose about SLOPE dB per band.
  • Band-pass — keeps a window about one band wide: at 24 dB/band the cutoff band is −0.8 dB, its neighbours −13 dB, two bands away −36 dB.
  • Band-reject — removes the band at the cutoff completely. The notch width depends only on SLOPE: at 6 dB/band it hollows out two or three bands on each side, at 24 its neighbours are only 1.6 dB down, at 96 nothing but the exact band is touched. With steep slopes, a cutoff between two bands (say 5.5) hardly notches anything — use whole numbers.
  • Resonance — adds up to +24 dB centred on the cutoff, falling off over about ±1 band. With a low-pass at 24 dB/band and RESO 12 dB, the cutoff band ends up at about +8 dB.
UNDER THE HOOD

The cutoff actually used for each voice, recomputed every 32 samples and smoothed sample by sample:

cutoff = CUTOFF + ENV AMT × mod envelope + LFO CUTOFF × LFO + VEL AMT × (velocity − 1) + (1 − KEY TRK) × log₂(261.63 Hz ÷ note frequency)

The mod envelope runs from 0 to 1, the LFO from −1 to +1 and velocity from 0 to 1. The note frequency includes OCTAVE, FINE, pitch bend and vibrato. The result is not clamped to 0–12, so modulation can push the filter past fully closed or past fully open.

6Position window

Atoms live at definite places in the cycle, so engine B can decide where the detail is allowed to exist. With the POSITION window you keep the coarse band 1 everywhere but let bands 2–11 sound only in part of the cycle. The result is a moving “focus” — a kind of formant or pulse-width effect that is locked to the pitch.

W(φ) = (1 − DEPTH) + DEPTH × bump(φ) bump = raised cosine of total width WIDTH, centred on POSITION
output(φ) = band 1(φ) + W(φ) × [bands 2 … 11](φ)

φ is the position inside the cycle. Because the window is locked to the cycle, the result is still perfectly periodic — it changes the timbre, it never adds noise or detuning.

High notes and modulation

A narrow window multiplies the sound by a short bump, which adds high harmonics. To keep this alias-free, the window is automatically widened on high notes: it is never narrower than 16 × note frequency ÷ sample rate of the cycle — about 9 % at C4 and 35 % at C6 (48 kHz). The minimum WIDTH of 5 % is only reached below about 150 Hz.

LFO POSITION moves the window up to ±180° — all the way round the cycle — and is only audible when DEPTH is above 0 %. The SCOPE shades the window and the SCALOGRAM draws it as a dashed line.

7Envelopes

Two ADSR envelopes run for every voice. The AMP ENVELOPE shapes the loudness; the MOD ENVELOPE moves the filter cutoff, by the amount set with ENV AMT in the SCALE FILTER (it has no other destination and ignores velocity — use VEL AMT for velocity-dependent brightness). Engine B adds a third, per-band envelope — the SCALE ENVELOPE — which shapes every band separately before the AMP ENVELOPE shapes the whole.

10Sub oscillator

The SUB OSC section, at the bottom left next to the wheels, adds a simple oscillator one or two octaves below every note — the classic way to give a sound more low end, from a warm bass foundation to infra-bass you feel rather than hear.

11Effects and master

The effects process the sum of all voices — wavelet sound and sub oscillator together — in this order: saturation → ping-pong delay → reverb → DC blocker → master volume → soft limiter. The delay and reverb mix knobs add the effect on top of the unchanged dry sound (there is no dry/wet crossfade), so raising them also makes the sound a little louder. The reverb receives the echoes of the delay as well.

  • Ping-pong delay. The mono sum of the voices enters the left channel; each repeat crosses to the other side and passes a gentle low-pass, so echoes bounce between the speakers and darken as they fade.
  • Reverb. A Freeverb-style stereo reverb (eight comb and four all-pass filters per channel) with fixed high-frequency damping; SIZE sets the decay.

13Sound-design recipes

Each recipe starts from a factory preset and uses only the controls on the panel. Values are what the panel shows when you hover over a control.

Drawbar organ

Start from: B · Octave Organ (or B · Init)
  1. PATTERN RAMP, WAVELET Daubechies 20, TILT 0 dB/band.
  2. Drawbars 1–7 at 100, 80, 60, 70, 30, 50 and 20 %; drawbars 8–11 at 0 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: ATTACK 10 ms, DECAY 100 ms, SUSTAIN 100 %, RELEASE 80 ms.
  4. LFO: SINE, RATE 6 Hz, PITCH 6 cents — or leave PITCH at 0 and use the mod wheel. REV MIX 20 %.
  5. For a percussion accent: SUSTAIN 60 %, BODY 20 s, BRIGHT 250 ms — the upper bands start loud and settle quickly, the body stays.

Try next: Haar turns every band into a square wave for a reedy organ; WALSH gives a hollow combo-organ colour.

Chip-tune Walsh lead

Start from: B · Chip Walsh
  1. PATTERN WALSH, WAVELET Haar, TILT 0 dB/band.
  2. Drawbars: 1 at 100 %, 2 at 50 %, 3 at 70 %, 5 at 40 %, all others at 0 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: ATTACK 1 ms, DECAY 200 ms, SUSTAIN 60 %, RELEASE 50 ms. VOLUME −6 dB.
  4. VOICES 1 and GLIDE 30 ms for chip-style slides.
  5. For a fast trill: LFO SQR, RATE 12 Hz, PITCH 50 cents (the pitch flips between −50 and +50 cents).

Try next: move drawbars 2 and 3 to change the Walsh “duty cycle”; add DRIVE 20 %.

Bell with per-band decays

Start from: B · Walsh Bells
  1. PATTERN WALSH, WAVELET Symlet 8, TILT −3 dB/band.
  2. SCALE ENVELOPE: ATTACK 2 ms, SPREAD 0 %, BODY 3 s, BRIGHT 0.4 s, SUSTAIN 0 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: DECAY 4 s, SUSTAIN 0 %, RELEASE 2 s.
  4. REV MIX 35 %.
  5. Play in the upper octaves: the bright strike fades within half a second while the body rings on.

Try next: RANDOM (try several SEEDs) for less regular, glassier bells; BRIGHT 0.1 s for a marimba-like strike.

Swelling pad with SPREAD

Start from: B · Mirror Pad
  1. PATTERN MIRROR, WAVELET Daubechies 20, TILT −5 dB/band.
  2. SCALE ENVELOPE: ATTACK 1.5 s, SPREAD +100 %, BODY and BRIGHT 20 s, SUSTAIN 100 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: ATTACK 1 s, SUSTAIN 100 %, RELEASE 2 s.
  4. UNISON 4, DETUNE 18 cents, STEREO 70 %. REV MIX 50 %, VOLUME −6 dB.
  5. Hold a chord: the body is there after about 0.4 s, the finest bands only after 6 s — the pad opens slowly without any filter.

Try next: SPREAD −100 %: detail first, body last — a reversed, breathy swell.

Impulse pluck

Start from: B · Impulse Pluck
  1. PATTERN IMPULSE, WAVELET Daubechies 4, TILT −2 dB/band.
  2. SCALE ENVELOPE: ATTACK 5 ms, BODY 1.2 s, BRIGHT 80 ms, SUSTAIN 0 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: ATTACK 1 ms, DECAY 1.5 s, SUSTAIN 0 %, RELEASE 500 ms.
  4. DLY MIX 20 %.
  5. Play: a bright click that turns into a rounder tone within a fraction of a second, like a plucked string.

Try next: Haar for harpsichord bite, Daubechies 20 for a softer pluck; CUTOFF 7 with VEL AMT +4 bands so soft notes lose their top bands.

Random glass with seeds

Start from: B · Random Glass
  1. PATTERN RANDOM, SEED 17, WAVELET Daubechies 8, TILT −4 dB/band.
  2. SCALE ENVELOPE: ATTACK 200 ms, SPREAD +50 %, BODY 6 s, BRIGHT 1.5 s, SUSTAIN 40 %.
  3. AMP ENVELOPE: ATTACK 300 ms, SUSTAIN 90 %, RELEASE 2 s. UNISON 3.

From square to sawtooth

Start from: B · Init
  1. PATTERN RAMP, WAVELET Haar, TILT −6 dB/band, all drawbars at 100 %.
  2. Hold a low note (around C2, where bands 1–9 sound) and pull the drawbars down one by one from drawbar 9: each removed band halves the number of steps. Drawbar 1 alone is a square wave (Figure 4).
  3. Put them back up and change TILT: −3 is brighter than a sawtooth, −9 darker, 0 a buzzy stack of equal squares.
  4. Switch WAVELET to Daubechies 20: the same registration becomes a smooth stack of octave sines — the steps were the Haar atoms.

Listen for: at middle C only drawbars 1–7 make a difference — see Where the bands are.

Focus sweep (LFO → POSITION)

Start from: B · Focus Sweep
  1. PATTERN RAMP, WAVELET Haar, TILT −3 dB/band.
  2. POSITION: POSITION 0°, WIDTH 30 %, DEPTH 100 %.
  3. LFO: SINE, RATE 0.5 Hz, POSITION ±180°. AMP SUSTAIN 100 %.
  4. DLY MIX 25 %, REV MIX 20 %.
  5. Hold a note and watch the SCOPE: band 1 (a square) stays put while the staircase detail travels around the cycle.

Filter pluck on an additive source

Start from: B · Init
  1. WAVELET Daubechies 8 (keep RAMP and TILT −6 dB/band).
  2. SCALE FILTER: LOW-PASS, CUTOFF 2, SLOPE 24 dB/band, RESO 6 dB, ENV AMT +6 bands.
  3. MOD ENVELOPE: DECAY 400 ms, SUSTAIN 0 %. AMP ENVELOPE: DECAY 1 s, SUSTAIN 40 %.
  4. VEL AMT +2 bands for dynamics.

Listen for: the classic subtractive pluck — but because each RAMP band is one octave partial, the resonance steps from one octave partial to the next as the envelope closes.

Legato flute

Start from: B · Soft Flute
  1. VOICES 1 (mono), portamento selector LEGATO, GLIDE 80 ms.
  2. Keep the preset’s scale envelope (ATTACK 80 ms, SPREAD +60 %): the upper bands bloom a little after the body, like breath.
  3. Play slurred phrases (press each key before releasing the previous one): the notes slide into each other without a new attack, as a flautist slurs.
  4. Detached notes re-articulate: each starts on pitch with the gentle attack and its own band bloom.
  5. For a breathier tone lower TILT to −12 dB/band; for more vibrato raise PITCH or use the mod wheel.

Listen for: slurs and articulation controlled by your playing alone. POLY would make every note glide; OFF would make every note jump.

Gliding organ chords

Start from: B · Octave Organ
  1. Keep the portamento selector on POLY (the default) and set GLIDE to 250 ms.
  2. Play a chord, release it, then play the next one: C–E–G, then F–A–C. Each voice glides from its own previous note: G slides to F, E to A, and C stays.
  3. Slow chord progressions work best; the pure octave partials of the Daubechies 20 bands keep the slides clean.
  4. For a more vocal effect try PATTERN MIRROR and a longer GLIDE (500 ms).

Listen for: an organ whose chords bend into each other voice by voice. With LEGATO the chords glide only while you keep a key down.

Pedal organ with a 32′ sub

Start from: B · Octave Organ
  1. Keep PATTERN RAMP, WAVELET Daubechies 20 and the drawbars; pull drawbars 5–7 down for a darker pedal sound.
  2. OCTAVE −1 and VOICES 1 (mono, legato) for a pedal line; GLIDE off.
  3. SUB OSC: LEVEL 60 %, OCT −1 octave, SHAPE SIN.
  4. Play a slow bass line between C2 and C3. With OCTAVE −1, drawbar 1 sounds like a 16′ stop and the sub like a 32′ stop — the low C of a 32′ stop is 16.4 Hz, the lowest note of the great cathedral organs.

Listen for: pressure rather than pitch below 20 Hz — the sub is not filtered, so the drawbars and TILT keep shaping the organ while the sub stays a pure foundation. Choose TRI if your speakers cannot reproduce it.

TIP

PATTERN, SEED, WAVELET and ENGINE rebuild the wave tables when they change. While playing in real time the new tables are built in the background and crossfaded over about 10 ms, so a ramp on these controls moves in small steps rather than as a smooth sweep. When Live renders offline (export or freeze), each change is rebuilt immediately, so the render matches what you automated. For smooth, continuous movement automate the filter, the POSITION window, the drawbars, TILT or the effect levels.

17Tips & troubleshooting

SEED is greyed out

SEED only affects the RANDOM pattern.

All drawbars up, but the sound is quiet

The mix is divided by the sum of the drawbar levels to avoid clipping. With TILT 0 and eleven drawbars up, each band plays at 1/11 of full scale. Use a negative TILT, fewer drawbars, or raise VOLUME.

The sound is harsh or thin

Check TILT: values near 0 or above make the fine bands as loud as the body. IMPULSE and RANDOM are bright by nature — use TILT −4 to −8, or a short BRIGHT time.

No sound in the web app

  • Did you click Click to start audio? The status bar must read Audio on.
  • Check the header VOLUME knob (double-click resets it to −3 dB) and your system volume. The meter should move when you play.
  • Check the patch: an AMP SUSTAIN of 0 % with a very short DECAY, all drawbars down, a low CUTOFF, or a SCALE SUSTAIN of 0 % with very short BODY and BRIGHT times can all make a note almost silent. Load B · Init to start from a known sound.

“Audio error: …” in the status bar

The browser could not start the audio engine. Use a current Chrome, Edge or Firefox (the engine needs AudioWorklet support) and reload the page. As a last resort, if a locked-down browser refuses pages opened from disk, serve the folder locally — for example run python -m http.server 8000 in the web folder and open http://localhost:8000/.

“The manual was not found” (plugin)

The MANUAL buttons open the manuals stored inside the plugin bundle. Install the complete WaveletSynthesizer.vst3 folder, not only the file inside it.

I hear two pieces at once, or the sound continues after closing

The web app plays in only one window or tab at a time: starting the audio in one pauses the others, which show “The synthesizer is playing in another window or tab, so this one was paused.” with a Click to play here button, and closing or leaving the page stops its demo and audio at once. If you still hear two pieces, or sound after closing, close the other window or tab of the app (or click its overlay to take the audio there), then reload the page.

No sound from the plugin

Make sure the MIDI track is armed (or its monitor is set to In), that MIDI from your keyboard reaches the track, and that the plugin’s VOLUME is up.

My MIDI keyboard is not found

Use Chrome or Edge, which support Web MIDI. Allow MIDI access when the browser asks; if the status bar says MIDI: access denied, re-enable it in the site permissions and reload. Devices can be plugged in while the page is open.

The computer keys do not play

Click on the panel first so that the page has the keyboard focus, close the manual overlay, and make sure no text field is active. On non-QWERTY keyboards, use the physical positions shown in the key map.

The right-hand drawbars do nothing

That is expected on middle and high notes: each band is about one octave of harmonics, and the instrument only plays harmonics that fit below the anti-alias limit. At C4 only bands 1–7 are played, at C6 bands 1–5; bands 10–11 exist only on the lowest notes. See Where the bands are.

Harsh, metallic or “folded” tones at the very top

The tables are band-limited per note, so aliasing normally stays above 19 kHz. Extreme settings — very high notes with OCTAVE at +3, deep vibrato or pitch bend at the top of the range — can still push harmonics past the limit. Lower the octave, or reduce PITCH. Narrow POSITION windows are widened automatically on high notes for the same reason.

Clicks

Very short AMP attack or release times (1–2 ms) can click, especially on low notes and smooth waves; 3–10 ms is usually clean. Changing PATTERN, SEED or WAVELET while a note sounds is crossfaded and should not click.

Squashed or distorted sound, meter at the top

Resonance (up to +24 dB), unison, big chords, DRIVE and the effect mix knobs all add level. The output limiter never lets the signal exceed 0 dBFS, but when it works hard the sound gets squashed. Lower VOLUME or the offending control.

High CPU load

CPU use grows with VOICES × UNISON and with the number of bands that actually sound. Lower UNISON first, then VOICES. Bands whose gain is (almost) zero are skipped, so steep low-pass settings and high notes are cheaper. In the browser, close other heavy tabs.

My sound changed after switching engines

ENGINE only swaps the engine section. The SCALE FILTER, POSITION, envelopes, LFO, voice and effects keep their settings — for example the CUTOFF 7 of “A · Init” also filters engine B. Load an engine’s Init preset for a neutral start.

My sound is gone after reloading

That is by design: the web app always opens on A · Warm Pad. Keep sounds with Save preset (stored in this browser) or Export (a file). Private windows, another browser or clearing site data lose the browser-stored user presets — exported files are safe.

I cannot hear the sub oscillator

Its LEVEL is 0 % by default. At −2 octaves on low notes the sub can be below 20 Hz, which small speakers and most headphones cannot reproduce: try −1 octave, a higher note, or the TRI or SQR shape, whose harmonics stay audible.

18Glossary

Additive synthesis
Building a sound by adding simple components — classically sine waves, here bands of wavelet atoms.
Aliasing
Harsh, inharmonic tones produced when a digital oscillator tries to play harmonics above half the sample rate; they fold back into the audible range.
Atom
One shifted, scaled copy of the wavelet, with a size (coefficient). One cycle of 2048 samples is described by 2047 atoms plus its average.
Band / scale
All atoms of the same size. Band b has 2b−1 atoms; band 1 spans the whole cycle, band 11 has 1024 tiny atoms. One band ≈ one octave of harmonics.
Band-limited table
A copy of each band layer with harmonics above a limit removed. The engine picks the copy that fits the note, so high notes do not alias.
Daubechies (DBn)
A family of wavelets by Ingrid Daubechies (1988). DBn uses 2n filter taps; longer ones are smoother and separate the octaves more cleanly.
Drawbar
A slider for the level of one component, as on a tonewheel organ; here one per band (octave).
DWT
Discrete wavelet transform. Here it is periodic (the cycle wraps around) and orthogonal (no atom duplicates another), so the 11 band layers add up exactly to the original cycle and the energy is preserved.
Haar
The oldest and simplest wavelet (1910): a square step up then down. Its atoms are piecewise constant, which gives stepped, digital-sounding results.
Infrasound
Frequencies below about 20 Hz: under the range of hearing, but felt through large speakers.
Key tracking
How much the filter cutoff follows the played note.
Legato
Playing a new note before releasing the previous one; in mono mode the envelopes then continue instead of restarting.
Mother wavelet (ψ)
The basic shape from which all atoms are made by shifting and scaling. The ATOM SHAPE display shows it.
Pitch-synchronous
Analysed and played one waveform cycle at a time, locked to the note’s period. Bands therefore move with the pitch: band 5 is always about harmonics 9–16.
PolyBLEP
A light-weight way of removing the aliasing of the sharp edges of a digital square or sawtooth wave; used by the sub oscillator’s square.
Portamento (glide)
A smooth slide of pitch from one note to the next instead of a jump. With polyphonic portamento, every voice of a chord glides from its own previous note.
Scalogram
A picture of a wavelet transform: time across, scale (band) up, brightness = atom size. The wavelet counterpart of a spectrogram.
Sparsity
Keeping only the largest atoms and zeroing the rest — the principle behind wavelet-based lossy compression such as JPEG 2000.
Sub oscillator
An extra oscillator one or two octaves below the played note, used to add low end.
Symlet, Coiflet
Variants of Daubechies wavelets designed to be nearly symmetric, which gives more balanced, less lopsided edges.
Taps
The length of the wavelet’s digital filter. More taps = smoother atom, better octave separation, less precise placement in time.
Unison
Several slightly detuned copies of each note played together for a thicker sound.
Walsh functions
Square-wave functions that switch between +1 and −1 in regular patterns; the digital counterpart of sines, used in some early digital synthesizers.
Wavelet
A short, wave-like shape with zero average that starts and ends quietly. Stretched and shifted copies of it are the building blocks used to describe a signal.

  • Engine A, Wavelet Subtractive: 6 source waves, 7 wavelets (Haar, Daubechies 2/4/8/20, Symlet 8, Coiflet 3), sparsity, scale filter (LP/HP/BP/BR, cutoff 0–12 bands, slope, resonance, key tracking), position window.
  • Engine B, Wavelet Additive: 6 atom patterns, 11 drawbars, tilt, per-band scale envelope.
  • Sub oscillator (sine / triangle / square, −1 or −2 octaves) for extra low end.
  • Shared: amp and mod envelopes, LFO, 16 voices with mono/legato, polyphonic portamento (Off / Poly / Legato only, Poly by default: every voice glides from its own previous note; Legato glides from the held note) with glide time, unison, saturation, ping-pong delay, reverb, DC blocker, soft limiter.
  • Band-limited (alias-free) playback; the C++ engine matches the JavaScript reference within 2.4e-7.

Presets: 32 factory presets (17 for engine A, 15 for engine B), from clean sounds to heavy effects, ordered with OktoPierre's 15 favourite sounds first (Square Mod 2, Wavelet Lead, Haar Bass, Edge Pluck, Focus Sweep, Soft Flute, Infra Sub Bass, Dub Echo Lead, Fuzz Bass, Laser Sweep, Warm Pad, Mirror Sub Bass, Sparse Keys, Space Choir, Square Mod), then the others alternating A / B and the two Init presets last (the order of the DEMO preset tour too; the VST3 stores the preset shown in its browser by name, so Live Sets saved with the earlier order show the right preset), including "B · Mirror Sub Bass", "A · Square Mod" and "A · Square Mod 2" by OktoPierre; the app and new plugin instances open on "A · Warm Pad".

Security fixes (code review of 2026-10-14)

  • Oscillator: a fundamental above the sample rate (e.g. note 127 with OCTAVE +2, or notes from G8 up with OCTAVE +3) made the VST read outside its wavetable and crash the host; in the web app it made the sound NaN until reload. The phase now always stays inside the cycle, and voices (and the sub oscillator) whose fundamental is above Nyquist are silent. Normal playing is bit-identical.
  • MIDI file reader (web "Load a MIDI file…"): lengths read from the file are limited to the data really present, so a damaged or hostile file can no longer freeze the page; tempo lookup is now fast for files with many tempo changes.
  • VST3 message attributes: null arguments and too-small string buffers from the host are rejected.
  • vst/install.bat: the folder path is no longer pasted into an administrator command line (the elevated step runs the same file again), so folder names containing ', & or parentheses are safe.
